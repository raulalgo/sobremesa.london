### Rambling 1. Thoughts on AI thoughts

Before hitting me with Geopolitics, my mid-life crisis hit me with Philosophy. It was one of those ironies of destiny. My grandfather spent decades teaching Philosophy in high school. Since I became a teenager he had been gently chasing me to wake up an interest, if not the whole field, at least on Plato, Aristotle and his beloved Athenian Democracy. Sadly, I only opened one of those books when he was too old to have a proper conversation with him about it.

It must have been when I was in the middle of that newly discovered rabbit hole that YouTube sent my way a video of an old man giving a Philosophy lecture in Google’s campus, challenging the brightest minds of AI to lose all hope of getting consciousness out of a computer. John Searle, a behemoth of late XX century philosophy wrote his Chinese Room in 1980. In 2022 he was still arguing its case with exceptional clarity.

The lecture is worth the hour it lasts. But if you want the TL;DR, give yourself a few minutes to digest this thought experiment:

> Imagine you find yourself in a room with two windows and a long list of instructions. From one of the windows you receive a series of Chinese characters. You don’t know what they mean, but following the instructions that you’ve been given, you eventually get to the set of characters that you should return through the other window. That way, assuming we have all the time in the world, the propositions that come through one window get their responses through the other. Once the conversation is over, would you say that you (or whoever is operating the room) can speak Chinese?

### Rambling 2. Skip intro

If cars were designed the same way we design software, it would go like this:  
— The user only has two feet but has to operate three pedals. Also the stick forces them to release one of their hands from the wheel. We need to automate shifting gears so the whole experience is more ergonomic. – the designer would say.  
— Ok, and how are we going to do that? – The PM  
— Well that’s for the engineers to figure out.

It is not enough that in digital we detached our work from the way our designs are built. It got so bad that showing interest on the technical part could be frowned upon — Look up “Should designers code” on Google or x.com if you want to get a taste. Luke Wroblewski, a classic of Design Twitter, not only believes that we should code, but remembers that we used to.

Like in every break up story, it is not just that design parted ways with code, but also the other way around.

This might sound alien to the youngest ones reading, but there was a time in which we would design on canvas, animate on a timeline, and the browser would be able to “play” it all just as we intended. The 2000s sounded like Nu Metal, and looked like a Flash website, layered with more effects than we know how to put together today. It may seem, if you ask an engineer, that slop didn’t require AI after all.

We are about to get noisy and loud again; many will roll their eyes; but it will be a return to technology enabling ideas and not the other way around. Nature is healing.

### Share. Designs we didn’t build

As the first results of my [Beauty as a Service](https://designsobremesa.substack.com/i/186234661/growth-pill-beauty-as-a-service) survey started to come in, the next move was clear.

> Hey, take results.csv and build a data visualization page that lets me explore the responses per team and per vertical. Give it a clean editorial style. Make sure it is not too noisy. Let the data speak for itself.

The result: elegant in the visuals and only a couple of prompts away from wowing the user. Where are we doing better? Who is harshest in the judgement of our own product? Where can we improve?

Even if the theme is not fully my style, or the company’s, I can ask for a tweak, or even copy the whole thing to Figma and edit it manually. These kind of workflows are no longer a promise. They’ve been part of our day to day for almost a year now.

Then there is the rush to record the screen and share with everyone the visualization I just built. The technologies I chose. The decisions I made. Because I did all of that. Didn’t I? Or am I just trapped in a good looking room pushing weird symbols back and forth?
