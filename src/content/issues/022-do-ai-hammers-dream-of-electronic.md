### Confession 1. A config to rule them all

Since I started vibe coding my way through some of my most tedious designs I feel like I’ve been wielding a hammer that made everything look like nails. Now, maybe because I’m looking at things from the next level up in [the ladder](https://designsobremesa.substack.com/p/021-the-ai-fluency-ladder), some of those nails seem to be misbehaving and I’m beginning to wonder if I should consider a screwdriver.

If as designers, even when we look at our own discipline, we stick to the good old problem-solution paradigm, we will never find the reason to move on beyond the solutions we use today into a potentially better one. The problems we have are very reasonably solved. None of this AI is helping me build and maintain my Design system, it doesn’t make my handover any easier, and I still have found no better way to do an exploration than a canvas. Why change?

It is when you move to the problem-vision-solution when you can start looking at things differently. None of the AI tools that I use every day are any better than [Figma](https://www.figma.com) at doing what Figma does best, yet, I rarely open it these days. A year ago I was all about vibe-coding as a design tool. I believed prompting an AI would get us designers closer than ever to code. Today, all I can think of is skills that can design like I do with me barely typing a prompt.

In a couple of weeks, Figma will be launching all their big ideas for the next year in their yearly conference—[the config](https://config.figma.com/). In the meantime, here are some ideas of where I feel the future of design tooling is heading so we can check notes once all the announcements are out.

1.  **Can I use it in the MCP?** whatever new feature you are presenting, this is going to be my first question. A couple of weeks ago, for example, they already introduced an [AI-enhanced style checker](https://www.figma.com/release-notes/?title=check-designs-catch-whats-off-ship-whats-right). I no longer care how it looks in the UI, I no longer want to be manipulating all that detail, I would rather have [Claude](https://claude.ai) leverage it directly.
    
2.  **Thinking locally.** One thing that [Pencil](https://pencil.dev) does really well is that it will connect to my AI account. That means no extra subscription but also no extra configuration. When I type in Pencil I have access to my memories, my MCPs, and my skills. I feel that Figma has been very protective in the past of that walled garden they created by having everything in the cloud. I hope they don’t get afraid of thinking locally, so we not only can vibe code directly our designs in Figma, but we can also vibe code scripts that will automate a lot of the day-to-day design gruntwork.
    
3.  **From pixels to context.** Claude doesn’t need that much design context to produce screens that are aligned with our Design System. In the future—meaning, already, today—we’ll need Design systems and tools that, while they remain usable by people, are primarily meant to be read by agents and models. Up until today, Figma has been the manual home to my variants, my styles, my modes, my tokens, my whatnots… That is no longer useful if it can’t ingest and contribute all that Design Context to every step of the product development cycle.
    

Silicon Valley gave us an AI and everything started looking like prompts. I may fail at predicting the future, but I’m pretty sure that there are more than prompts to be hammered in this brave new world.

### Rambling 1. Into the backroom

Next time you feel that culture peaked right around when you hit your twenties remember [Kane Parsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kane_Parsons). Back in 2019 he was only 13 when a creepy picture posted online moved him to grab a camera and a computer with [Blender](https://www.blender.org)—a 3D modelling software distributed for free—to create one of the strongest 21st century myths.

It’s only taken 7 years for Parsons to take that world to [the top of the US box office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backrooms_\(film\)), in the highest grossing film by the always clever [A24](https://a24films.com).

A fresh new take on horror and aesthetics through empty corridors, VHS recordings, and an unsettling white noise. Also a collective movement, full of forums where people will contribute the liminal spaces they’ve encountered or imagined. Don’t lose all hope just yet. There was (a back)room in the market for fresh and bold ideas.

We are lucky Parsons was successful because we get to run into his work. He heard [his own voice](https://designsobremesa.substack.com/p/012-the-sound-of-our-own-voice) and stuck to his guns. He could never have been wrong.
