### Rambling 1. In four levels

In the time I’ve been working with AI in my design practice I’ve seen too many people decide to “do AI” but struggling to unpack what that really means. In [a post from April](https://designertom.substack.com/p/the-4-levels-of-ai-fluency) that didn’t get the attention it deserves, Tommy Geocobroke that down in levels:

1.  **AI as a better Google:** AI used for basic research, summaries, and information retrieval.
    
2.  **AI as a builder of outputs**: creating specific prototypes, drafts or scripts. Most of us are probably here.
    
3.  **AI as personal infrastructure:** This is where you start scaling yourself up through AI. You are building skills, or tools that will save you time on your own work and processes. Potentially shareable with other members of your team so you are less of a bottleneck
    
4.  **AI as labor:** Here we close the loop using AI to call all those skills and tools from level 3.
    

In summary:

> A Cursor prototype is level 2. A skill that runs Cursor for you is level 3. A Slack agent that runs the skill while you’re in a meeting is level 4.

Here’s what I’ve been missing from these levels until we started applying them at Viooh: they are not just levels, they are a ladder we can climb. When you do that, one feature—or maybe a bug—emerges: from the level below, everything above feels like a content creator screaming the latest hype desperate for views; from the level above, everything below feels like a toy.

I’ve been raving about vibe coding as a design tool despite being late to that party. I was sitting in level 1 convinced I understood what this was about, dismissing all those announcements from [Lovable](https://lovable.dev/), or [v0](https://v0.dev/) as a cute gimmick. I moved on to level 2 once I was ready, I guess. I didn’t choose the moment. Ignacio Rovirashowed me the right example at the right time and that’s when I moved forward. Since then, that world of prompts enhancers, or “the best prompts for”, has only got less and less meaningful.

I’ll help you climb that ladder. One level at a time and at your own pace. Whenever you’re ready.

### Level 2. Azote

In modern Spain, Castile & Leon is not only one of its largest regions, but also one of the most aged and sparsely populated. With everything in the country revolving around Madrid and Barcelona, one could end up thinking that this is a land where nothing ever happens. Yet, the last month has been refreshing like no other thanks to a collaboration with [DIME](https://www.dimeasociacion.org/) (C&L’s professional designer association) and Valladolid’s [Azote](https://azote.es/en/home/).

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!jvni!,w_2400,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fc3931112-e9aa-42c9-bd54-ab50b76e7b2c_5734x2470.png)

Alberto and Virginia run this branding studio with a portfolio full of projects that speak for themselves. If they were based in a bigger city they would probably be making more heads turn. They are wonderfully strong at something I’ve been able to experience first hand: The concept.

I was lucky to collaborate with them in the branding for DIME’s annual conference: [Meseta Crew](https://mesetacrew.dimeasociacion.org/). In a moment in which it was especially important for all of us to show (not tell) that something indeed happens on the plateau; also in the middle of all the vibe-coding craze with animations and shaders taking over our screens; in the middle of all that, they chose to be loud by using very tiny mouths: the mouths of little birds.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!I5bQ!,w_2400,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F1ebfd5c9-172b-4f3a-b652-b38fef9003bc_7412x3092.png)

It is worth coming back to the AI ladder. With my limited coding skills, not in a million years I could have pulled off the website we launched a couple of weeks ago. Vibe coding, prompting natural language to Claude plus a bit of hand-eye coordination is all we needed to turn Azote’s flock of birds into a proper Castilian murmuration.

The [Flash-moment](https://designsobremesa.substack.com/p/013-whos-in-the-room) we have ahead of us, being able to translate our imagination into code is pure Level 2. Very exciting on its own but also with two levels more to go beyond.

### Level 3. Headless design

Figma has been a great tool at easing lots of us into a level 2. Vibe coding to replace traditional designing was accelerated thanks to [Figma Make](https://www.figma.com/make/). [Replit](https://replit.com/), Lovable and v0 existed before but Figma brought that mechanic right to where our design systems were.

My bet is that whoever breaks level 3 to us, will do it in a headless fashion. In an agentic product development workflow you’ll get a system that connects (via [MCP](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/)) to your tools: [Linear](https://linear.app/) for business, Figma (or [Pencil.dev](https://pencil.dev/), worth a check) for design, and your codebase for styles, patterns and components. A system that uses a series of skills to plan, implement, code review and test a feature. That world of skills and tools talking to each other is Level 3 saying hi. The most relevant surface of a design tool is no longer the GUI. It’s the MCP.

Why would a product design team need to think in terms of level 3? At Viooh we are struggling with a thin design team that becomes the bottleneck for our product development. I’ve spent my last couple of weeks writing skills—or should I say vibe coding them more precisely. Skills to identify reusable components in a design, to follow our design system, to explore multiple options without interrupting me every 5 minutes, etc. Interesting already for my designers but thought to be used by our PMs.

AI is not the path to get rid of designers (we can’t afford any fewer) but to augment how much land they can cover. If we teach AI to design like us—if you missed Diego Zaks interview in [017](https://designsobremesa.substack.com/p/017-the-light-ahead-210) it would be a good time to go back to it—the designer can cover more ground, lightly review these first passes and focus the energy on elevating and polishing whatever is working. The design tools we know and love today can be run in a headless fashion—without even opening the UI—and our designers will do less pixel pushing and more design critique.

I’m still figuring this out and I’ll share progress as we go. If you want to test the waters, start pushing yourself to go from prompts to skills, from deliverables to processes.

### Coda. Level 4

While I was coding the Meseta Crew website, sharing updates with Alberto, he would tease me, threatening with the million change requests a little studio like Azote would get working on a website like that for a client. How much time do these small and medium businesses spend in this endless back and forth, half polishing exercise, half last-minute indecisiveness before pulling the trigger?

Now imagine next time this happens to them, when they log in in the morning, they not only have the client’s email with 15 change requests, but a response from Claude, including 7 pull requests with the most trivial items ready to be reviewed and pushed to production with a click.

That’s level 4 and it is achievable today. You will need no experts or expensive courses, and it will be the difference between editing 30 typos on a restaurant’s website and a long wandering sobremesa under the Castilian sun.
