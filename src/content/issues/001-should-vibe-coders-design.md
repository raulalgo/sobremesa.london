### Growth Pill 1. Embracing our weaknesses

Like most of you, these days at [Viooh](https://www.viooh.com/) are also days to review how the last year went. Where we did well and what was more challenging. Seems difficult to have a chat with your manager in which you are questioning what you could have done better without immediately adding some version of “I’ll get working on that”. Seems inevitable, specially if we look around at this always-hyped world of LinkedIn, to get caught up in the I-have-to-do-more illusion. Kobe Bryant woke up at 4am to squeeze in another training session? I can learn a bit of code because technical awareness is not my strongest suit. Or I can commit to detailed and beautiful documentation of my design systems even though I hate writing.

While this attitude is great to see in a direct report here is my take as a design manager: **Don’t be afraid to embrace your weaknesses**. The path to seniority is not one of being able to do everything yourself but one of bringing the right people together. If documenting is not your strongest suit, yes, working harder on it is a possible answer; another possible answer could be recording a Loom video and posting in on Notion or Confluence; or talking to your colleague who is actually good at docs to see how you could help each other by dividing and conquering.

Find your own way, but remember: career progression is as much about what you know as it is about having the right people to ask when you don’t.

### Rambling 1. Not-Big Tech & Open Source in the face of AI

In 2025 I’ve heard a lot of concerns around AI, Design and Intellectual Property. However this video by Theo Browne shines a light on a different issue hitting harder the most popular open source projects.

[Tailwind](https://tailwindcss.com/) is one of those projects (you can’t call them “little” anymore) that have been so successful that now run the styling of a very good chunk of the internet you see every day (I won’t dare to give a percentage but I’m willing to bet on a very high number). Since they give their key product away for free, they’ve had to be very creative in the recent year to find ways to make money out of their expertise so they can grow the team that maintains and updates Tailwind to keep it going. Now the explosion of vibe-coding LLMs is putting them at risk of dying because of their own success. Tailwind got so big pre-AI that now it is the styling default used by any LLM whenever you prompt them to code anything. This is making them, both, even bigger but also losing their main source of revenue through sales of templates.

For those of us that still believe in Open Source, it is a case to follow closely, since it highlights the challenges that Not-Big Tech faces in the red seas of AI.

### Rambling 2. The dawn of the Design Engineer.

But, wait a second. Wasn’t this a DESIGN sobremesa? Why are we talking about libraries, code, and open source? Well, those who know me or have worked with me, know how much I believe in technical awareness as a key skill for the success of a digital product designer.

\- Should designers code?

\- A bit. More than your average designer but without pretending to be a Frontend engineer.

This would have been my take pre-AI. Now, LLMs open a very exciting opportunity. So many of you have landed on this as a no-code technology. Now you can go from natural language to live code running on the browser. I’ve even heard of some design teams daring to push code to production.

We can go even further. Designers now have an opportunity of, using their LLMs as a crutch, to dip their toes in the role of a Design Engineer. Reserved before to those unicorns that were solid Frontend developers that learned strong foundations of good design, AI now opens the road in the other direction, enabling great designers to create work directly in the shape of tags and styles.

This may not be a change for everyone, but for those who dare, I expect you will become very valuable assets in the market mid to long term.
