### Rambling 1. Eco-es from the past

A couple of weeks ago, when I found myself name-dropping Umberto Eco’s [Apocalípticos e Integrados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apocalittici_e_integrati) ([Apocalypse postponed](https://www.amazon.co.uk/Apocalypse-Postponed-Perspectives-Umberto-Eco-ebook/dp/B084KJQXNQ/ref=sr_1_1?crid=2EPVA961S8K44&dib=eyJ2IjoiMSJ9.1gr4wbaFdhYk-AnP4_ks4nxbxKg9QkSmIGyU6rRK--jGjHj071QN20LucGBJIEps.1RROMLbU3y33AA6V71-qKK-5W13W-w7k1DWrZditq_Y&dib_tag=se&keywords=apocalypse+postponed&qid=1774541379&sprefix=apocalypse+postpon%2Caps%2C260&sr=8-1), in English) while commenting a Substack post, I felt I was overdue in opening Amazon and getting a copy of the book. Although it was written in the 60s in the context of the explosion of mass media and pop culture, by the 2000s, when I was in Comms school it was still relevant. Just last week, Spanish influencer, Carla C., reminded me that I still haven’t done my homework by [dedicating her last post to it](https://delisandwichdecangrejo.substack.com/p/el-apocalipsis-con-wifi):

> There is something deliciously arrogant in his premise (Eco’s): if a cultural product is suitable for everyone, it is no longer culture, but an ideological mash that’s been pre-digested so nobody chokes.
> 
> [El apocalipsis con Wifi, by Carla C.](https://delisandwichdecangrejo.substack.com/p/el-apocalipsis-con-wifi)

With AI, the cultural neighbourhood of tech, once very quirky, exclusive, and maybe a bit hipster, is now showing the first signs of gentrification. Narratives justifying our practice on the basis of taste, craft or solid engineering now have many of us grabbing popcorn while our friends in journalism yawn from the back of the room in front of a movie that they’ve watched quite a few times in the last couple of decades.

Everything seems about to change, and it probably will (it always does), but I wouldn’t be surprised that once we are on the other side, we realize that everything is still pretty much the same. See you in a couple of months, for the reading club.

### Rambling 2. Splitting in two

If you’ve made it this far reading, give yourself a break from the thirty AI-assisted-design announcements made last afternoon and watch Tommy Geoco’s: The Industry is splitting in two.

He does something I consider very healthy at this point. He does the meta task of thinking of the sides rather than taking one himself. Whether you are excited or terrified by what’s ahead, it is great to have a reminder that the feeling of the other side is as real (and I would dare to say, as rational) as the other one.

### Rambling 3. The reports of our death

Designers being cooked with any new Vibe-coding related launch has become so widespread that is almost turning into a meme. [Yesterday, however, the meme turned into people actually trying to make a point, with stats that show how in the post-AI world engineering and PM hiring has increased while design remained stagnant](https://x.com/lennysan/status/2036483078374433237?s=20).

Because Internet is the Internet, a thread has a second life with all the quotes. From [“designers are useless and they had it coming”,](https://x.com/soleio/status/2036512905785659508?s=20) to [“just give this statistic some time and we’ll talk”](https://x.com/jenny_wen/status/2036524711727825048?s=20). All very intense, black and white, and also completely inconsequential in your day to day job because, fortunately, the Internet is not the real world.

The future of design won’t depend on what a handful of opinionated avatars, trying to farm engagement post online. Look with your own eyes, think with your own head, and trust everything you’ve learned and you’ll be ok. And in the dark days of doubt, remember that at the end of the first quarter of 2026 a company decided to launch a portable cassette player that they couldn’t call Walkman.

### Coda. Open doors

I write this newsletter from a belief that design is the coolest thing one could think about. I also believe that if I could learn to design, anybody can. Actually I also believe that everybody designs, some of us just happen to do it professionally. I’ve seen many takes that try to build some gatekeeping around design: a this-is-serious-stuff-and-you-should-be-careful-with-it attitude. That doesn’t resonate with me. I believe in bringing as many people as possible on this journey; that’s where I write from, even if that puts the law of supply and demand at odds with my own future.
