### Rambling 1. Secret Formula

— What kind of car would you like to drive?

For many years, I’ve used this question with students and colleagues to explain what “Experience” is.

Some people may think big and comfortable. How easy is it to buckle my kids in? How many suitcases can fit in the trunk? Can we squeeze the crib in?

Some may go for something more lightweight, closer to the ground, with a noisy engine. Something that looks for a specific road, rather than a destination. Something for a bit of weekend excitement.

And then there is a narrow band of people who would think of something different. Mostly for the thrill, but a few chosen ones because of their job. Who wants to drive a Formula 1?

A Formula 1 is not intuitive, nor comfortable, nor ergonomic. It is an equation in which the user—an extremely specialised one—will always be happy to deal with more complexity if they get speed in exchange. Changing my sitting position to almost horizontal so my weight is closer to the ground? Done. Adjusting seven dials through the lap to shave 3 more tenths? There you have it. Taking my hand off the wheel at 300km/h to redirect some weird airflow in the cockpit? Sure, why not?

Having designed most of my career in SaaS or B2B, I find this the best metaphor. We design tools for very specialised users, who will be happy to learn something difficult if that saves them 100 clicks at the end of the week.

### Rambling 2. Honestly

—There is nothing here that isn’t about utility and form.

[Jony Ive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jony_Ive)’s studio, [LoveFrom](https://www.lovefrom.com/), and Ferrari presented this week the [new Luce](https://www.ferrari.com/en-EN/auto/ferrari-luce), throwing a wrench in any attempt I might have had at planning this newsletter. [Last time I went through this](https://designsobremesa.substack.com/p/005-my-favorite-take-away), I left it at the metaphor. I’ll try to be a bit more specific today. The media tour they are doing is flooding my timeline with two key messages: this is very Ferrari and very Design. [I’ll let you Google what’s been said by those who know what “being Ferrari” means](https://en.ilsole24ore.com/art/ferrari-stock-market-collapses-montezemolo-risks-destruction-a-myth-AIWx5TID).

Back to Design, [Ive says the line above as part of a long interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K-o0r2zSgCE) in which he goes through every single commonplace he’s been repeating for decades in his attempt to be the 21st century’s [Dieter Rams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dieter_Rams). The problem? He says that at the wheel of a car with gearshift paddles but no gearbox.

Electric cars probably allow you to simulate a gearshift behind the wheel—I’m sure they can add the brief drop in power you get between gears too. They will even play an electric-y roar when [Lewis Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_Hamilton) goes flat out. I’m trying not to be narrow-minded. It all might be a legit choice. At least I hope we agree that those elements are put there not just for pure utility and form, but to create an experience based on simulated artefacts.

> “one, coherent, singular design (…) the design is very, very different (…) you are not going to be designing an interface because it looks groovy, it’s got to work”

0 to 100 designs per minute in the blink of an eye. Nothing new. Pure atmosphere. All form and little meaning. Do you want to stress test it? Think of the opposite of any of those statements. Would any be meaningful? Neither are these. Fair game, but very short legs.

When the designer has more space than the design, the product is not the design but the designer.

### Growth Pill. Drop the metaphor

— Next time, drop the metaphor.

Last time I used the F1 story in an interview, that was the first piece of feedback I got from the interviewer. I had something similar at my current company. That time I was using chairs. Designing our app is like designing a chair, we need to account for ergonomics, production but also beaut—a quick hand move signalled for me to move on to the next slide; he knew exactly where I was going.

This goes beyond talking to execs. Drop the metaphor: period. If whoever’s listening is old enough, they’ve heard it a million times. If they are younger than you, they probably won’t care. Even if this doesn’t make sense, just let the pendulum swing. The world of narratives is about to implode under its own weight.

New Ferrari? Here you have a 10-part series, 19 interviews, and lots of good vibes. After that we’ll move to all the 2-minute bits clipped from the originals. If this wasn’t enough, then you’ll get all te reposts on X or LinkedIn with more genuine outbursts of emotion saying that this is the best video ever done. Three days ago it was the World Cup with all those hyper-produced squad-announcement videos; same cycle. Tomorrow it will be something else.

I’m exhausted and I’m just a cranky old millennial; good luck with TikTok’s zoomers.
