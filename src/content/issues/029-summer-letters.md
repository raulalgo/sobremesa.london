### Rambling 1. Typing for a reader

If we were to look at the bits that make the cover of this issue or a file with the full text of _[El Quijote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Quixote)_, they would both result in indistinguishable soups of “random” ones and zeros. Yet when we run each of them through the right software all those bits are going to be decoded into the right colours or characters showing they were never random. We could even open the image with a text editor and see the gibberish that results from reading those pixels as letters.

I related a lot to Nicolás Prieto’s substack this week, [El dilema de la caja de texto](https://innterface.substack.com/p/el-dilema-de-la-caja-de-texto), since he shares a very similar journey to the one I’ve been going through lately. The excitement of designing interfaces through a text prompt, but missing the direct manipulation we could do with canvas-based tools, while still feeling incredibly slow compared to the speed of exploration you can reach with AI.

> When we prompt, we bind ourselves to the model’s interpretation of our words. Writing long prompts is a tedious task, far more so than attaching a design or a screenshot. So we naturally give in to the impulse to be less detailed and less precise than we could actually be designing on our canvas.
> 
> [El dilema de la caja de texto](https://open.substack.com/pub/innterface/p/el-dilema-de-la-caja-de-texto?r=d1dwl&selection=a1dbcd79-37e3-4249-a90d-006fed1625ae&utm_campaign=post-share-selection&utm_medium=web&aspectRatio=instagram&bgColor=%23020617&textColor=%23ffffff). N. Prieto

That weirdness of realising that my work has turned into typing interfaces into a machine resonated. Is this what we are supposed to do now? Is this the end game? [You know I don’t think it is](https://designsobremesa.substack.com/p/017-the-light-ahead-210).

In the last quarter a new workflow has emerged. I would write a few bullet points in a [Linear](https://linear.app/) ticket, use [Claude](https://claude.ai) to refine it like a PM, then feed it all back to Claude itself to generate some designs I could evaluate—sometimes in a canvas tool like [Pencil.dev](https://pencil.dev/)—. The canvas would let me decide and directly manipulate those final touches and when designs were ready I would ask Claude to “write a design spec” back into the ticket.

To those of you that design in tech, I recommend you try this exercise if only to get a glimpse of the wall of gibberish-in-plain-English that the thing generates. Completely impossible for a person to read but to the exact level of precision your LLM needs.

These artefacts may be written in perfect English grammar yet they are meant to be read by a machine. Maybe that’s why we are so sensitive to AI-like texts on LinkedIn, because, despite everything, we can still tell it wasn’t written for us.

### Rambling 2. Notes on the Synthesis of Form

I have to thank Nicolás because his article also reminding me that I had [Notes on the synthesis of form](https://www.hup.harvard.edu/books/9780674627512), by [Christopher Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Alexander), sitting on my desk waiting for the right peg to recommend it as a maybe not so light summer read

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!XUhh!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F7d02d63f-59fe-42ea-8353-d5a8077269a9_3840x2160.png)

For a while I’ve been arguing that design predates all our theories about it. I’ve written multiple times about how it used to be that we designed what we could build and not the other way around like today. I’ve tried to make sense of this idea through [medieval architecture](https://designsobremesa.substack.com/p/010-quod-natura-non-dat) or by [differentiating design and Design](https://designsobremesa.substack.com/p/004-fast-and-loose-with-the-d-word). Someone’s credentials may not say “Designer”, the process may not be formally named “Design”, but things that have been thought of have been inevitably designed.

Alexander’s book explains it better, and you won’t have to read the whole thing; two chapters, thirty pages, will do: The difference between the Unselfconscious and the Selfconscious process. He looks at the different shapes traditional societies have settled on to build their own homes. Long before Design or Architecture are formulated, way before anyone can even name their own discipline, these constructions are full of nuance and cleverness—of the kind of a roof that insulates well while remaining accessible when it needs fixing—. This unselfconscious process is the result of:

> the form-maker not only makes the form but lives in it.

After this, eventually there is a moment in which the process becomes selfconscious. The form-maker now is an individual that shapes the whole process. Disciplines and academies emerge. Individual designers are supposed to come up with solutions that are better than what was learned by generations. Rules and canon are written down becoming something to think with or against.

Fixes to design problems used to take generations. The more selfconscious our process gets, the faster we churn through problems. Faster even than we can assess how right or wrong our solutions have been. That’s why non-designers keep designing. They haven’t had contact with all those generations of corrections—haven’t we departed from that too?—but they still live in the forms they create.

### Coda 1. Farce Two

The piece I got at the [Summer Exhibition](https://designsobremesa.substack.com/p/023-interlude) earlier in June already arrived in my mail. Farce Two is a simple photo of a kids’ playground at night. Googling the name of its author, Spanish abstract painter [Santiago Alemán](https://www.santiagoaleman.com/), brings a website and Instagram full of art and work. No bios. No long and elaborate narratives on the rationale behind his work. Pure show, don’t tell. If you look at the photo you’ll see how it screams [about my latest obsession](https://designsobremesa.substack.com/p/026-missing-context).

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Wwkz!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ff34b30e1-e0b3-43cd-808b-427b43e57819_3840x2160.png)

Beyond the picture itself, the beauty of the [Summer Exhibition](https://www.royalacademy.org.uk/exhibition/summer-exhibition-2026): the opportunity to buy art at the [Royal Academy](https://www.royalacademy.org.uk/), receive it in your mailbox, just for the price of a meal.
