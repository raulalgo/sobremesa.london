### Share. Viooh Week

Yesterday during Viooh week, our big yearly company get-together, I was lucky to sit on yet another panel on AI. Moderated by Liz Dowling and sitting with my colleagues,Emily Bird, Emily Bearn, Jad Hangari, and Marcelo Fachinelli. Probably the most interesting thing has been seeing how the excitement on AI is much more balanced and less extreme than the narratives we were discussing last week. Most of us have found points for efficiency gains but we are also aware of its limitations and how necessary it still is to have a human driving the creative part of the process.

Lots of change ahead of us, no doubt about it, but the more we use it, the less we fear it will take over our jobs.

### Rambling 1. Will Smith vs Ben Affleck

While I was still reflecting on Ben Affleck’s opinion of AI’s output being shitty, I ran into the following post comparing side by side the progress of video-generating AI through the example of Will Smith eating spaghetti.

This allows for reflection not only on the progress of the technology but on the nature of artistic value. Because, while tech enthusiasts obsess on every step taken in the direction of realism and accuracy, looking at those videos through the creativity lens we could wonder:

— Which one is more iconic?

### Growth Pill. Beauty as a service

Design should be about much more than making things look nice. We learn this in school; we remind each other constantly in design conferences; and we get very frustrated when a client asks us to "make something pop". Yet, a couple of weeks ago, I found myself asking someone at work:

— If I give you a scale where 0 means ugly and 10 means beautiful, where would you put our product? (…) Let’s start writing down everything we need to do to make it a 9 or a 10.

While beauty shouldn’t be the final goal of good design, it should be its byproduct. We are not supposed to design for ourselves, yet sometimes we know very well what needs fixing. A non-designer might not know (or care about) what good design truly is, but when I ask people in my company about their pet peeves with our own product, I can get a very good indicator of the things that we should be fixing.
