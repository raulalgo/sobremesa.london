### Rambling 1. Let’s call it fútbol

This newsletter is a weekly space to explore the intersection of Design, AI and pop culture. Love it or hate it, the biggest pop culture event in the last week had a bunch of my compatriots as the protagonists:

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Y14c!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fd06f25a9-6927-456d-8100-06456d229be9_1024x576.jpeg)

These guys make it into the Sobremesa not because I still have my face painted red and yellow, but because they share with [Bad Bunny himself](https://designsobremesa.substack.com/p/006-a-tale-of-two-chairs) and [Kane Parsons](https://designsobremesa.substack.com/p/022-do-ai-hammers-dream-of-electronic) what has become my favourite trait of this team: their composure. The composure that’s hijacking the zeitgeist. The ability to do what you do while remaining calm and professional. Not just what you do, but what you know is best—[that little voice inside](https://designsobremesa.substack.com/p/012-the-sound-of-our-own-voice)—, even if the world around you tries to convince you you’re wrong. A déclassé music style, passing the ball with no superstars, quirky CGI shorts, or whatever you’re working on next.

We say back home _de gustos no hay nada escrito_, but at least this time, the artists flew back home with a star embroidered in their jersey that will never go away.

### Rambling 3. Verbatim

As great a nostalgia device as the Walkman is—it’s even [made a guest appearance here before](https://designsobremesa.substack.com/p/011-cooked-to-a-crisp)—I don’t have such an intimate bond with it. I was a teenager in the interim between that and the first iPods. I got a [discman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discman), while some of my friends were trying their luck with the [MiniDisc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiniDisc). Yet, none of those have an aura equivalent to the sense of awe and freedom that the first walkman must have meant.

That moment came for me with the CD burner, instead. [Nero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nero_Burning_ROM) firing up in my computer and a pack of 50 blank [Verbatims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verbatim_\(brand\)) ready to be burnt. An addictive super-power that, once unlocked, had you hunting for the next project (sounds familiar?): a nu-metal mixtapeCD, a movie or a videogame. No matter where you stand on the IP-piracy conversation, all those CDs changing hands, crowding filing folders, labeled, hand-written or with the cover poorly printed, trigger—like a smell—a memory that sends me to a here and now that no multinational could ever have planned.

Branding may be about to face a similar moment. Those of you that have spent some time in Spain this summer must have noticed the booming b-market of football jerseys during the world cup—all that used to be CDs when I was young—. The super popular [away kit Adidas designed for Spain](https://www.adidas.co.uk/spain-26-away-jersey/JN4397.html?pr=lookbook&slot=1) would never have been so ubiquitous had the only way to acquire it been paying the 100 plus euros that the official one cost. These bootleg markets have worked so well that my cynical self is even wondering if this has always been part of the strategy.

This is where pop culture meets AI and Design this week. As the anticipation builds up to see what Adidas releases as the new official kit for the world champions with that much-desired second star over the crest, some AI-generated interpretations of it have gotten very popular online. So popular, in fact, that they are already being sold.
