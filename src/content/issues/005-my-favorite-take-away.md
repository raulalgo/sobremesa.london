### Confession. It’s not you, it’s me

I have to confess all the buzz and material around Jony Ive’s last creation for the interior of the Ferrari Luce hasn’t resonated with me at all. Not talking about the designs themselves but about that mythology and aura that is being created around it. Especially those four books worth of research delivered with the final project.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!FH0y!,w_2400,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F518759e4-70fd-4d70-96d5-2bf3edc65c44_4000x3200.jpeg)

I can’t avoid looking at this and seeing the design equivalent to a 3-Michelin-starred restaurant doing very technical stuff, and a small cook-to-guest ratio, charging £800 per menu. Great for them. Probably they’ve worked hard to get to that point. But it leaves me wondering about the rest of us and why it is that we always end up so fascinated by the most elitist expressions of our own profession. At the end of the day, no wonder you can do much more with big clients and budgets, but why are we not hungry to see smaller studios come up with clever solutions when their budgets are much tighter?

Stretching the metaphor: If Jony Ive is a super chef running the best restaurant in the world, what is design’s cheap but delicious takeaway shop around the corner? Or what would be design’s equivalent to an amateur like me cooking a paella for some friends on a Sunday?

### Growth pill. On Non-design

We had Adam Davis and Parveen Downer yesterday in the office from [Colab Cohorts](https://colabcohorts.com/), kicking off one of their training programs to work with our teams in best product practices, collaboration, and tighter discovery loops. In the opening session, a question came up on prototyping: Is that something the designer should do? Or the PM can do it too? (The borders between product and design: one of my favourite topics).

If you’ve been reading me, you’ve heard me many times talking about how transformational it has been to move from designing just in Figma to using a vibe-coding tool like v0. While it was initially a rollout for the designers, we quickly made the decision to onboard the whole product team too. Wouldn’t we be creating noise? How could design keep control of our work if product can go now and prototype directly what they want?

More ideas means more opportunities and that should always be welcome. I’ve said before that Product’s expertise is all about understanding the problem, while [designers are experts on the realm of solutions](https://designsobremesa.substack.com/i/185418803/growth-pill-1-designers-super-powers). That doesn’t mean that we can’t cross-contribute, it just means that everyone is probably better at what they have specialised in. A PM vibe-coding their vision for a given problem can be a great starting point — one of many that the designer would eventually consider. Have your designer in the loop, discuss the problem with them and together you’ll make the most of each other’s ideas.

### Rambling. No-prompt AI

If you don’t follow Danny Saltaren, you should. Now that he’s posting in English you have no excuse. This week I saw the following line in one of his posts about [Mito AI](https://mito.ai/), his newest project (bold is mine):

> [We just made this in minutes.](https://designsobremesa.substack.com/i/185418803/growth-pill-1-designers-super-powers) **[No prompting skills needed](https://designsobremesa.substack.com/i/185418803/growth-pill-1-designers-super-powers)**[. No technical headaches](https://designsobremesa.substack.com/i/185418803/growth-pill-1-designers-super-powers).

The funny thing is that Ignacio Roviragot me into vibe-coding after a no-code module with Danny. Prompts were our ultimate no-code coding tool. Yet, prompts are just the latest abstraction, another tool that we need to learn and master to make the most of the machine. Whatever future lies beyond, it makes sense that **no-prompt** becomes the natural next step.

Keep an eye on them.
