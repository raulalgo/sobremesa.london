### Confession. El rey del pop

[Last week’s earthquake on Design](https://designsobremesa.substack.com/i/187752409/confession-its-not-you-its-me) allowed me not to talk about the whole asteroid that rocked the world that same week with Bad Bunny’s Half-time show in the Super Bowl. I’ll leave the over-analysis of every detail to the millions of words that have already been written on the topic and use this space for a little confession.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!1lut!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F31fafdc2-d3dc-473a-bc64-5b0f89f7291a_1280x720.jpeg)

I’ve hated reggaeton since I could recognise that music genre. I’ve rolled my eyes every time that _[Dame mas gasolina](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CCF1_jI8Prk&list=RDCCF1_jI8Prk&start_radio=1)_ played in the club while at the same time my body shyly danced to the beat. I would hide behind irony to signal that there is no way I would enjoy that music but that I still wanted to be part of the party.

Yet, I see with envy Bad Bunny sticking to the guns of his _declassé_ music to the point of being the most streamed artist of the year four times since 2020. Not so long ago, people like Shakira or Ricky Martin had to sing in English to make it to the global stage and now [this guy has everyone tearing apart his Puertorican accent](https://x.com/burnoutinho/status/2022344546055487874?s=46&t=a-nyOQRLlO3KfsM7OH7f-w).

Is there a lesson in this for designers? Is there a low-end design that we are frowning upon—one that a young student could use to build the great design of the next decade?

### Rambling. Lo prohibido

There are objects that are like the little arrow in the FedEx logo. Once you see them you can’t believe how it is possible there was a time that you were so oblivious to them. I’ve gone through that journey in the last week with a forbidden object.

According to Wikipedia, [the city of Basel, in Switzerland, banned plastic chairs between 2008 and 2017 “to preserve the beauty of the cityscape.”](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monobloc_\(chair\)#Social_reception) We love so much to talk about the great objects that we forget that the terrible ones have also been designed. This one, in particular, not only has been designed by someone. It has a history, features in [lists of the most influential chairs](https://contemporarylynx.co.uk/10-interesting-chairs-and-their-stories), its own [documentary](https://www.imdb.com/es-es/title/tt14663106/), and its own name: **the monobloc.**

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!RPgz!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe762b650-e018-4da1-8962-f6061efabab9_1024x409.jpeg)

[“So ubiquitous that if you see one on a beach you can’t tell if someone brought it there or if it is a floatsam from another part of the planet”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JwApFGwMIMQ). It is the kind of object that can’t translate its commercial success to Mount Olympus of great design. Instead, it has us debating between the vice of waste and the virtue of democratization. Yet, despite it all, some artists are finding the way to do something with it like [Bert Loeschner transforming them into a chess set](https://inhalemag.com/bert-loeschner-art-everyday-plastic/), or a young musician from Puerto Rico building a whole atmosphere with two of them in the cover of his last album.
