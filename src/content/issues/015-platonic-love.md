### Confession. The challenge

Let’s pick it up where we left it last week. I use AI to write this newsletter. Well, more precisely, I use AI as the _editor_ of this newsletter. Every single one of these lines has been hard fought in front of a keyboard. I will write the three bits in three different files. Once I’m done I’ll ask Claude Code to prepare it for Substack. This triggers two tasks:

1.  It will combine all of them in a single file and do some high level grammar check. I’ll insist it doesn’t change my voice. It will only flag clear spelling mistakes and my “spanishisms” but I won’t even remove all of them.
    
2.  Then, the most fun. It will read all the previous issues of the newsletter and give me some editor notes. It will flag my strong themes and my weak ones. None of the stuff it points out is shocking, but it is accurate enough to make me act on it and give it one last round of polish. My favourite part of the process.
    

The only issue with an LLM reading everything you wrote before publishing the next one is that it will remind you the stuff you had already forgotten about, like last week:

> “The promise you made in [004 - Fast and Loose with the D-word](https://designsobremesa.substack.com/i/186971420/growth-pill-1-the-popsicle-stand) (’I’ll come back in a latter issue and give you my best go at what it is that we do’) is still outstanding. Fourteen issues in, the “popsicle stand” has never been followed up either close it or acknowledge you never will”

Coincidentally, it is just now, with the weight of my own words tapping me in the shoulder, that I feel the first writer’s block of this whole series.

### Rambling. The decision

Design is what I do, it is my uni love, it is a constant feeling of awe with the way things are and the way they could have been. It is the vertigo of promising something you don’t know if exists, and the thrill of knowing you will eventually get there. Ultimately, designing is fun.

—Yeah, fine but I’ve asked you what design is, not how it makes you feel— It could be Claude, or my grandfather too.

Ok, let’s try again. Design is the necessary step between the intent to do something and that something existing. A joiner building their own table, they could even go straight to the wood and that table would have a design. A teenager looking at the posters in their room, leaving the Power Rangers behind and choosing Ayrton Senna as their next hero is designing too.

—Cool, but I’ve asked you what design is, not when it happens.

Design is looking at an object and realising that you are touching someone else’s idea. It is the criteria and the taste it takes to solve a problem according to a vision. It is the experience to envision something that is functional and beautiful. Or, even better, beautiful because of how functional it is.

—I’ve asked you what design is, not what it requires

It is a problem-solving process!

—So what is design? the problem or the solution?

Design is none of the above. It is not the problem, nor the solution, not even the vision that tells us where to go. But it requires all of it. The same we could say about the taste, the craft, the business case or the constraints. All add layers and richness to the process, but not a single one of them is necessary. Design can be tasteless, sloppy, rookie, and economically unsound and it would still be design. Yet if we are going to make a living of our design we need most if not all of those layers.

Because, ultimately, to design is to decide.

### Rambling. The definition

Maybe this should be another confession. When Claude challenged me I didn’t feel dread but excitement. I even wonder if that’s why I opened this Substack in the first place. All the question “What is design?” needed to be finally resolved was me, an overconfident Spaniard willing to write down what is crossing his mind. The dread came as soon as I started writing it all down.

No rambling in these 15 sobremesas have taken so many discarded lines. It should be to no one’s surprise that putting words to what is a creative and mental experience feels immediately underwhelming. Yet I had to put myself through it.

The funny thing is that I don’t want to waste much time thinking about the design that I do. Designing screens, flows, user journeys, experiences, software, etc. Debatable, yes; but also relatively straight forward. It is when I think that I took at least two design classes in my computer engineering days: Database design and Operating Systems Design. None of that had anything to do with UI. So how do we address the ubiquitousness of the term while at the same time drawing its contours. What do the designs of databases, fashion and brands have in common?

My aha moment came during the pandemic and it has guided me ever since. Much more precise than anything I can put into words, Spanish Philosopher Ernesto Castro uploaded a conference given in [Valencia in 2020](https://open.substack.com/pub/designsobremesa/p/009-drain-the-slop?utm_campaign=post-expanded-share&utm_medium=web) under the title “The philosophical Idea of design.” Not the easiest approach to the topic but for sure the most useful:

> Designing is accessing the world of ideal forms to apply them to matter.

Design was, after all, nothing but another footnote to Plato. As a teenager I couldn’t make sense about Plato’s concept of the world of ideas being real (not that I was paying much attention, tbh). What do you mean the idea of a triangle is a real object? Where? Someone in the clouds? Design, like every creative discipline, shows us the answer if we are willing to listen.

Design is looking at an object and realising that you are touching someone’s idea and their decision to make it real.
