### Rambling. Claude has left the chat

The signs have been there for a while. In [005](https://designsobremesa.substack.com/p/005-my-favorite-take-away), Danny Saltarén, maybe inadvertently, used “no prompting skills needed” as a selling point for his product. [Brian Chesky, CEO of AirBnb, put it in these words](https://x.com/tbpn/status/2027466333617852878):

> AI is in the MS-DOS era. (…) Think about the phone, do you want a chat to get the weather?

The last punch that knocked me out last week came from [Diego Zaks](https://x.com/diegozaks), VP of Design for Ramp. In his podcast with Tommy Geoco, he is no longer asking:

> You don’t want to chat to your bank or your expenses.

I hope you don’t miss this one:

Bolting a chat to your product, your website or your message is just a too short-sighted use of AI and I think I’ve felt it coming since I got an Alexa. Back then, my only use case was listening to the Spanish radio to fall asleep. Since the thing had no buttons, the trigger to my routine was yelling “¡Buenas noches!”, which when you’re in your mid-thirties, living on your own, in a foreign country is the opposite from a delightful experience, no matter how well the sounds are designed there.

When LLMs solved the problem of natural language we were sold a future that looked like [Her](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_\(2013_film\)). A world in which we would all gently talk to our machines which would be able to create the most intimate and transformative experiences—”Is the billing address the same as the delivery address?”— Lovely. Tell that to the kid that in the nineties would hang in panic as soon as he felt that it was the answering machine and not his friend who had just picked up.

Language is natural among people, of course, but there is nothing natural (let alone human) in a machine other than the fact that they came out of our minds and willpower. I think I side with those that feel that this is not the final form of this boss. I don’t fully see how LLMs and natural language could make UIs and interfaces unnecessary. It rather feels as if the right UI with AI infrastructure will remove the need of talking to our machines.

Remember that video I shared a couple of paragraphs above? The one you probably saw was 90 minutes long and thought, “yeah, maybe later”? If you are trying to figure how AI and design come together you should know I’ve sent it as homework to the designers in my team because it holds a bunch of keys to unlock this post-conversation AI future:

-   The strongest skill one can acquire regarding AI is asking Claude (or your intelligence of choice) how to do whatever you want to do. There is no other secret. If you (or the people in your team) are not doing that already, this is the best first step to get building stuff that is, first of all, useful for yourself. At [Ramp](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yazRO0jRTag) they’ve gone as far as making this item one that they assess in every performance review.
    
-   Worth stressing it one more time: “Would I want to talk to \[whatever you are working on\]”. Be honest about whether your latest project resists the test. Mine, probably doesn’t. Once we have acknowledged that, we can start thinking where in our workflows (or our users’) can we start harnessing data (and language) that will save us the prompting. — Nota para los hispanohablantes: _harness_ va a ser la próxima palabra de moda. No me la traduzcáis por “arnés”. Esto no va de atarse a nada. Igual un poco de domar los datos, como se doma un caballo. Google Translate os dirá “aprovechar” pero los “aprovechamientos de datos” suena fatal. Si me preguntáis, yo tiraría con “Cosechadoras de datos.”
    
-   Design, traditionally, has been a bottleneck. We get a brief, think about it for a week or two, come back with ideas, feedback, you know. All of the above applies to our products but also to our practices. Ramp’s model for design is one in which AI has been trained to turn every PM and every developer (or every builder) into a 7/10 designer. “Good enough to pass our critique.” When the stuff has been in production **and is working** they get a designer involved to make it a 10/10 or an 11/10 experience. I can’t think of anything else since Friday.
    

### Memory. Cruz Novillo

A few years ago, the Spanish agency [Summa](https://en.summa.es/) got entangled in a political controversy due to the redesign of Spanish Postal Service logo (Correos). They were accused of charging too much for a few touches on a logo that remained the same. I remember explaining to my dad: “The old logo is the closest Graphic Design can have to a cathedral, it is great they did so little.”

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!FMnR!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F0be2803e-1c18-4245-9509-e74f8a733c0a_1000x1000.webp)

[Cruz Novillo, the man behind that cathedral](https://tienda.cruznovillo.com/gb/logos/500-print-correos-color.html), passed away last weekend. He leaves behind a body of work that graphically dressed the transition of a country into the bright future that had been dreamt for decades. Logos, plenty, but also flags, and money itself. All this complemented with a career in plastic arts that stands on its feet and would even round out some of his corporate identity projects. When RTVE did a documentary on his career they just titled it: “The man who designed Spain”

Now it is on us, those who come after, to see past the nostalgia to maintain and keep this legacy fresh and current for the designers to come.

### Share. Lux

“If you haven’t listened to the new album, this is the best way to do it,” were my sister’s words as she was taking me to her second time seeing Rosalía‘s tour Lux. I won’t pretend I have anything to say that is even close to what her music and show is.

Action on the stage happens to be captured on camera and played on two big screens that frame the whole thing. Portrait, of course. If you think this is not what live music should be or how proper video should be played, she couldn’t care less and we should be grateful for that.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!NEgA!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F8b3dd756-8299-454a-92e8-65dc168ddc92_2376x1714.png)

A few issues ago I wrote about [Bad Bunny, sticking to his guns, singing in Puerto Rican until the whole world dances to his tune](https://designsobremesa.substack.com/p/006-a-tale-of-two-chairs). He could only do this standing on the shoulders of the giant that is Rosalía. Yesterday at the O2 Arena, in London, this show opened bouncing from ballet, to opera, to the ravest techno, to reggaeton. For a pause, an interlude with Semana Santa drums while two flamencos _doblan palmas_ at an inconceivable speed. Ah, yes, and that’s when Cara Delevingne walked onstage to a confession booth with Rosalía herself.

When you thought her references ended in the Caribbean, the south of Spain and her mediterranean homeland, the show ends with a techno version of a _botafumeiro_ –I’ll let you Google that one, but you should put Galicia in your to-visit list.

These lines are foolish. Pretending to capture her art and her show in a Substack is foolish. Pretending that I need to introduce you to Rosalía is foolish. If you don’t enjoy her music, at least accept and celebrate where she’s taken us. My only advice left? Download Spanish on your Duolingo, I guess.
