### Rambling 1. Love-Hate-Design

Last week, my timeline was taken by mentions and praise for yet another vibe-coding tool: [Variant UI](https://variant.com/). For the little that I’ve been able to try, this one seems focused on generating several variants for a given prompt so the user can choose a direction among them and refine further. You can imagine what comes next, February 2026, the digital product designer is cooked… again. Yet among all that hype, a very blunt question:

Interestingly, both sides of this tweet agree on the same thing. This new AI-enabled “browsing” can’t be what “Design” is. Can it?

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!aE_M!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F4c5ece56-bfb9-4a08-8972-acb62eb0f75d_3456x2234.png)

If you’ve ever talked to one of our colleagues that designed before computers you must have heard of a world of typesetting in letraset, fotocopying, and a more manual process (some might dismiss it by calling it “artistic”). If designing is just the technique we use to design, then maybe we stopped designing decades ago, when we replaced all those materials with a screen, a point, and a click. Or maybe, painting on a piece of paper or browsing through AI generated variations we are still doing something that can be referred to as “Design”.

### Rambling 2. When at the table do like the tablers do

If you can read only one thing today, go immediately and enjoy carlosprnt’s latest post on [My Design Side](https://open.substack.com/pub/mydesignside). (Yeah, I probably should have started by that. Sorry). I haven’t read many posts that capture so well the dissonance between what we say about design and what we actually do when designing.

> I’ve had this strange feeling for a while: I hear more and more people talking about design, and I see fewer and fewer people actually designing.

One would expect that by now we are done with wondering about our role and our seat at the table just to immediately wake up from that dream by reading this last couple of ramblings. It is so easy to feel trapped between those encouraging us to push every pixel perfectly (with a vector editing tool if possible), and those that think that visual design is something almost déclassé next to the wonders of strategy, value and KPIs.

One thing is sure, we won’t be able to use our seat at the table to give design a louder voice until we are able to explain what the h\*\*\* Design does.

### Growth Pill 1. The popsicle stand

I’m not going to hide from my own challenge. Just let me use a little tale first. I’ll come back in a later issue and give you my best go at what it is that we do:

A business walks into a design studio:

– I need some good design. Prime cut.

– OK, what for?

– I need a stand to sell popsicles in the arctic

While my product friends may be having cold sweats thinking of “What market are we tapping into?”, “How many popsicles are we going to sell?”, or “What problem are we trying to solve?”; a designer could ask “How many people are going to work on this stand?”, “Do you want the stand to be heated or are they going to be geared?”, “How frequently will you relocate the stand”, “How much stock do you want to keep in the stand?”, or “How do you expect the customers to get here?”.

Design is an exercise in solving a problem through our own vision of the future. As unpopular as this opinion may be, this can be done disregarding whether the thing that we are designing makes business sense or not. If you disagree with me, then:

1.  That’s great. Leave me a comment. I would love to talk more about this.
    
2.  Check the story of the Airbus 380, and whether its design had anything to do with its demise.
