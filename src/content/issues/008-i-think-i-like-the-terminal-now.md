### Rambling 1. El fin del producto

If you speak Spanish and are into Product Design (previously UX) you must know who Javier Cañadais (And if you don’t, you should). He has a decades-long career in which he has designed and trained designers, always from an approach of the utmost excellence. No compromises.

In the last issue of his newsletter [De Ulm a Cádiz](https://open.substack.com/pub/deulmacadiz)he shares a very intimate take, that is worth every second. In El final del producto (The end of product), Javier, fascinated as a user but terrified as a Design educator, shares his estrangement with the meaning of AI crashing through this discipline that he loves so much. Really worth the listen, and if you can’t understand Spanish, run the [transcript](https://www.deulmacadiz.com/p/el-fin-del-producto?showTranscript=true) by Google Translate.

There is still a long conversation ahead of us, and we seem to be splitting already in _[Apocalittici e integrati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apocalittici_e_integrati)_, just like Umberto Eco described back in the 60s, and I feel I keep landing on the integrated side. I haven’t seen yet a single LLM that can think like a person does. Products will disappear, companies will pivot, but there will still be a gap between the intent to do something and that something existing in the world, and the best gadget to do this that money can’t buy will still be hosted in our head.

### Rambling 2. Terminal-a-prompter

If last week I was wondering whether we’ve come full circle on the issue of making things look pretty; this post by [@sekachov](https://x.com/sekachov) made me wonder whether we’ve been doing anything but precisely that.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!IX_s!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F638207c8-da3a-40c0-917e-7aa426598e00_1196x1240.png)

I have to confess, Claude Code in the terminal feels unnecessarily good to use. It is also true that just yesterday a coworker was recommending their plugin for VSCode: “It is the same tool, but looks nicer”. Designers can solemnly tell you “Form follows function” to then move on to openly hate and make jokes on one of the most successful software products in history: Microsoft Excel. If you really want to go down the rabbit hole of ugly but powerful, try to find a power user of VIM in action.

None of these tools are trying to sell you anything new, but to make you faster and better at your job. One may think they may not be an example of beauty but they are still excellent in their design. You may even wonder if that quality is precisely what makes them beautiful.
