### Rambling 1. Good artists copy, great artists steal

A month or so ago, X.com’s timelines were taken over by the rumour of Israel’s Prime Minister’s death. A series of accounts picked up on one of the frames of a televised speech in which it could (allegedly) be seen a hand with six fingers. A damning sign of an AI hallucination. If the video is fake, speculation can run wild.

We were promised a world in which we couldn’t trust our own eyes. In which any unimaginable claim would be backed by a photo or video indistinguishable from reality. Yet, it takes us no time to tell when an image is impossible. The engineers optimised for every pixel being in the right place but in the end context gives fakes away.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!lPU0!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F7c40dd23-6ca9-405e-abf9-b67b1f2e450b_1024x576.jpeg)

We never got Netanyahu’s BBC obituary. His team kept publishing video after video trying to put an end to it. They were countered with more AI slop. You are counting with your fingers while you give a speech? Yeah, but look at this weird flap in your ear. Oh, you went out to a cafe to make yourself seen? Here you have, the Ayatollah deepfaked in that same place. What else do you have?

Nothing more (and nothing less) than a disinformation campaign in the midst of a war. Instead of a world where we would never be able to pick up a lie, we were given one in which nothing can be true anymore.

### Rambling 2. Own your dashes

And yet, we can be given away in a dash:

> Standard computer keyboards and typewriters include only one dash: the hyphen. Any normal font of text type, either roman or italic, includes at least three. These are the hyphen and two sizes of long dash: the en dash – which is one en (half an em, M/2) in width – and the em dash—which is one em (two ens) wide
> 
> The elements of typographic style, Bringhurst

People are not familiar with this typographic nuance nor with how to use it in their computers. For LLMs, on the other hand, everything is a number. They can pick the right dash at no extra cost and they always make the right choice thanks to their training. The conclusion many are reaching: if there is an en-dash in the text, it was likely spit out by a machine and copied as is.

In my [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/), where I write this newsletter, I have installed the **[Smart Typography](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/smart-typography-plugin-curly-quotes-em-dashes-ellipses/19484)** plugin which, among other things, will automatically convert a -- to a – and a --- to a —. Google Docs and, according to Claude, Microsoft Word also have those shortcuts built in, and so does the Substack’s editor (at least for en dashes). Even in your iPhone you can turn on the **Smart punctuation** setting and you will be able to send orthotypographic tweets and WhatsApp messages.

[I won’t paste three more pages of Bringhurst to explain their use](https://archive.org/details/elementsoftypogr0000brin_z4a6/page/10/mode/2up); you just need to read the headings:

-   Use spaced en dashes – rather than close set em dashes or spaced hyphens – to set off sentences
    
-   Use close-set en dashes (…) between digits to indicate a range.
    
-   Use the em dash to introduce speakers in a narrative dialogue.
    

Now, with all the information, yours is the answer. Will you deliberately type the wrong symbol to save you the embarrassment of a false accusation; or will you own even the dashes in your text and accept we will all be eventually accused of using AI?

What would your designer do?

### Growth Pill. Vibe coders screenshot

I started this year (and this newsletter) convinced that 2026 would see the dawn of the design engineer. I believed that my duty to the designers in my team was to teach them just enough of code to supercharge their abilities. Make sure you understand what Tailwind classes are; how HTML is structured; how a React component looks like. LLMs will take you 90% there so you can tinker with the last 10%.

I thought we had to learn to talk like developers. I’ve told my prototypes to use Tailwind, shadcn and visx; my new portfolio to use Astro and three.js. And then, last week, with my colleague Kamil:

— What is the best way to get the same components from our production tool? Can we install them through npm? Should I just copy the files?  
— Just share the folder with Cursor and tell the prompt to implement everything using the same components and styles. Put it in AGENT.md so you don’t have to prompt it every time

That was it. I prompt an experience and it’s built in our visual language straight away. The process now looks more like making a collage. Where we had designs, I threw a Figma Link. Where we didn’t, I would just paste a bit from Linear, Claude or whatever UI I’m looking at. [One tweet worth of inspiration](https://x.com/vittorioretrivi/status/2041299652939542657?s=20) and two hours later I’m trying to describe how realistic drops of rain behave when sliding down a window.

All of a sudden I’m done pretending to be an engineer. I’m no longer talking about frameworks or library choices. Not much of a shock there – that’s been most of my career. But even design systems, and pixel-perfect specs feel now a thing of the past.

Good artists copy; great artists steal. We’ve done that a million times. The difference now is that my screenshots no longer end in a mood-board, they go straight to my prompt.
