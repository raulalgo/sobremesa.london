## Rambling 1. Mediterranean letter salad

Valencia, the third city in Spain, is not only lovely and Mediterranean but it has done things so well on Design that I’ve been able to turn my weekend getaway into content for this newsletter.

On Monday I had the privilege of sitting down for an horchata with two heavy hitters of Valencian Design. On one hand, [Kike Correcher](https://www.linkedin.com/in/kikeco/), [ADCV](https://www.adcv.com/) and [READ](https://designread.es/en/home-2/)’s representative in [BEDA](https://beda.org/) (Bureau of European Design Associations) for almost a decade, and a fellow G42 member for their [MADres project](https://madres.beda.org/es/glorious-42/). On the other, Xavi Calvo, who was the CEO for [Valencia World Design Capital in 2022](https://www.wdcvalencia2022.com/) and for the [Foundation that has carried the flame once the year was over](https://fundaciodisseny.com/). If you need an excuse, in June you’ll have [Paradis](https://paradisvalencia.com/), a single day of conferences that will leave you with plenty of time to eat paella and stroll through the beach.

Keeping track of the world of design associations is definitely not user-friendly, but that doesn’t undermine the good work they do every day – a reminder that being a designer is not just about a good looking portfolio and happy clients. You can also apply your skill to design itself — wherever you are reading this from. When you meet Kike and Xavi you realise how “easy” it can be to do something as difficult as making your hometown world design capital.

## Growth Pill. AI-ing from the driver’s seat.

One of my big pet peeves with AI is insisting on having the LLM do the thinking for us. That, to me, is the great difference between something worth looking at and plain old slop.

Don’t get me wrong — AI slop is impressive. The images, texts and websites that we can generate by just prompting an AI model with natural language will never cease to amaze me. But when judging what remains on my screen after that feat of engineering, slop is slop, and then there is everything else. If you are a creative considering whether to integrate AI in your flow or not, remember your job is still the same — you just have a new tool now.

As a designer, there are two challenges I struggle with (and enjoy) in my process: the empty page and the execution. When someone brings me a brief, first I need to get as much information into my head to tear that problem apart. There is always a time of vertigo, while my page is still empty, that eventually resolves into some sort of “vision”: a little _aha_ moment in which I see what I want to do. The second big challenge is translating that vision to its final form, whether it is on a piece of paper or on a mobile screen. AI is excellent in the second one, but can’t replace your good judgement for the first one.

Be in charge. Own your work. Use AI as your tool or you’ll become theirs.

## Rambling 2. I want it T.O.T.

At the end of 2025, I went to [Badajoz to take part in a panel as part of one of the big design conferences in Spain](https://13enad.designread.es/). While at that point I was already an enthusiast about what vibe-coding brought to my design practice, on the image front, I saw nothing else but slop.

Then, [Ausias Pérez](https://www.instagram.com/ausias/?hl=es) from [T.O.T](https://tot.studio), a quiet guy coming from no other place than Valencia, made me reconsider. Almost an hour of portfolio presentation. No Design Theory. No catchy slides. No super crafted narrative. Just project after project, image after image, video after video — all of it peppered with frequent: _“We used AI for that part too.”_ Impeccable and futuristic. With such a strong creative direction that the AI vanishes behind his hand.

Don’t miss them. Don’t miss Valencia. And don’t blame me if I become a bit of a maximalist after this
