### Rambling 1. After config

Right before my break, as I was rambling about our [AI fluency ladder](https://designsobremesa.substack.com/p/021-the-ai-fluency-ladder), I included in my [wish list](https://designsobremesa.substack.com/p/022-do-ai-hammers-dream-of-electronic) for [this year’s Figma annual conference](https://config.figma.com/) my hope to shift from pixel-pushing to context management. I’m far from catching up with all the sessions in the event, but when Spanish youtuber [Raúl Marín](https://www.youtube.com/@RaulMarin_Figma) shared his favorite talk by Figma advocate and creative coder [Jake Albaugh](https://jakealbaugh.com) I was very pleased to find out they kicked off with this line:

> Today we are going to be talking about the importance of good Design Context

It is an almost intimate walkthrough of the day to day of someone that is using AI as a tool in their creative process while making sure they—and not the LLM—remain in control. Among that plethora of words we are finding to target the x-factor the designer keeps bringing to the table, Albaugh goes for ‘consideration’.

The most meaningful take away is that Design Context doesn’t have a clear surface yet. In 30 minutes we see some configuration .json files but also some FigJam Boards meant to tell agents how to use all that context. Details are strategically kept away—it may seem that some future launches are coming this way—but I’m willing to bet that if we were able to lift the hood, those agents are not only using that context but also writing it, or vibe-coding it, in the shape of all those text files that are going to be fed to an LLM so it does what we want. AI here is not an idea generator, nor an oracle—it is a very precise tool that can be expertly operated to get things done.

But even more significant is what is NOT there. I’ll treat it as old news from now on, but I’ll underline it one last time so we don’t miss it: this presentation doesn’t waste a minute talking about prompts or chats. It has moved on to context and agents.

### Rambling 2. Butterfly Effect

We learned two summers ago that our daughter Isabel, 18 months old at the time, gets motion sick. If leaving our home was already a ritual my childless self could have never imagined, this added a whole new set of items to our checklist. Water? Change of clothes? Bags? During our holidays, in the [Vueling](https://www.vueling.com/) that would fly us from Ibiza to Málaga, the bag in question was this one.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Gu_R!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F8b03e0f6-195a-4dd2-9a41-0c25ae2fd400_3840x2160.png)

I found it quite surprising that no-one in the whole process of designing all the applications for Vueling’s cute and fresh brand, starting in whoever typed this in [Illustrator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adobe_Illustrator) and ending in the stakeholders that gave the sign off, was capable of thinking how this reads when you are actually feeling more than butterflies in your stomach.

But the true problem cuts deeper. It’s 20 years ago that I started my Advertising degree. This type of branding that finds its way to a clever joke on every application was seen as a breath of fresh air. We would eat it like popcorn, and celebrate it online. Agencies and consultants have built careers preaching on this. Putting communication and narratives at the center, more important than the products themselves, it may seem.

20 years later, this feels like the backroom of branding: an undetermined space filled with artefacts that were once means to an end and along the way turned into a bunch of purposeless ends scattered around.

### Rambling 3. OK, millennial

In contrast to that _[horror vacui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horror_vacui_\(art\))_ that branding has been displaying for the last two decades, [Liminal Spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liminal_space_\(aesthetic\)) offer a clean and direct contrast. Take [Jared Pike](https://www.jaredpike.art/)—who [I read is a product designer](https://www.aquamagazine.com/builder/pools/article/15123573/dreaming-of-digital-pools), by the way—and his [Dream Pools](https://www.jaredpike.art/projects/dream-pools), a series of hand-made 3D scenes of impossible and purposeless pools that are completely devoid of human presence.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!GGnv!,w_2400,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F6728631c-24da-4d77-b1e5-eb4a122c8d95_5564x2160.png)

Dreamy, and eerie, these spaces don’t need a backstory. They just are. They exist because someone imagined them. Look at them, pay attention. Even if they don’t make you jump off your seat, they may be pointing at what’s coming next. Sooner or later, everything that—now old—millennials feel comfortable saying or doing will be negated, and these are the first steps. Yours is the choice to approach them with genuine curiosity or just so you can yell at the clouds.
